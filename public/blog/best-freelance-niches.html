Niches

How to pick a freelance niche you can prove

Updated 19 October 20267 min read

An older version of this page ranked niches with “Zenvylo scan data” and average rates clients were willing to pay. That data does not exist. There are not thousands of customer scans behind those tables, and a website fetch cannot see what someone will pay you. This is the replacement.

Start from a problem a stranger can check

The niches that fit this product are the ones where the gap is on a public page. A café with no HTTPS, a salon with no booking link, a plumber whose site does not load, a shop with no page title. You can put that sentence in an email and the owner can look.

A niche built on private facts — their profit margin, how many calls they miss, how they feel about their logo — needs a conversation. You can still sell it. You just cannot open with a number you did not measure.

Match the service to the check

  • Web design. No site, a parked domain, no mobile viewport, a very slow response, a DIY builder left alone.
  • SEO. Missing title, missing meta description, no structured data, stale footer year, slow response.
  • Local visibility. No website on the map listing, no phone, no tap-to-call once a site exists.
  • Booking and reception. No scheduling link and no contact form. You still have not measured missed calls. Say what is missing, not what it costs them.
  • Ads and analytics. No analytics snippet, and a page so slow or insecure that sending paid traffic at it is a weak idea. The missing pixel is the fact. The “wasted ad spend” is your opinion — label it that way.

Zenvylo weights those checks differently for each preset service. A missing booking link matters more if you sell scheduling than if you sell logos. That weighting is in the code, not a survey.

Pick a place you can actually serve

A niche is a service plus a kind of business plus a place. “Websites for independent cafés in the city I can visit” is a niche. “Digital marketing” is not. Scan one town. Read the first ten measurements. If most sites are already fine, that town is a weak place for that service, for you, this month. That is a real finding about those ten sites. It is not a finding about the whole country.

Price from your costs, not from a chart on the internet

What to charge depends on your time, your expenses, and what a specific client agrees to. A blog table of “average rates” is someone else’s guess. Keep a note on each lead of the number you actually proposed and, if you win, the number you were paid. After a handful of closed deals, that column is yours. Until then, do not quote a market rate you cannot source. See the pricing guide for a way to think about retainers without pretending we surveyed the market.
